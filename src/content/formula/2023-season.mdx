At the very front, 2023 rarely left much room for suspense. **Max Verstappen** won 19 races, took his third consecutive World Championship, and helped **Red Bull Racing** win all but one Grand Prix.

The interest was in everything happening behind him. **Fernando Alonso** put Aston Martin on the podium from the opening race, McLaren went from the back of the midfield to fighting near the front, and **Carlos Sainz** produced the one weekend Red Bull could not control.

## Race Calendar

The calendar was meant to contain 23 rounds, but severe flooding in Emilia-Romagna forced the cancellation of the Imola race. That left **22 completed Grands Prix**.

Las Vegas joined the schedule as a Saturday-night race, while the Sprint format expanded to six weekends: Azerbaijan, Austria, Belgium, Qatar, the United States, and São Paulo.

## Drivers and Constructors

Red Bull, Ferrari, and Mercedes kept the same pairings, but several of the moves behind them shaped the season.

- **Red Bull**, **Ferrari**, and **Mercedes** all retained their 2022 pairings.
- **Fernando Alonso** left Alpine to join **Aston Martin**, replacing the retiring **Sebastian Vettel**.
- **Pierre Gasly** moved to **Alpine**, forming an all-French line-up with **Esteban Ocon**.
- **Oscar Piastri** made his Formula 1 debut with **McLaren**, replacing **Daniel Ricciardo**.
- **Nyck de Vries** took a full-time seat at **AlphaTauri**, while **Logan Sargeant** joined **Williams** as a rookie.
- **Nico Hulkenberg** returned to a full-time Formula 1 seat with **Haas**.

AlphaTauri also changed drivers during the year. Daniel Ricciardo replaced Nyck de Vries from Hungary, then **Liam Lawson** stepped in when Ricciardo broke his hand and scored points in Singapore.

## 2023 Season Analysis

The winners' table is almost entirely blue. **Verstappen** won 19 of the 22 races, while **Sergio Pérez** added victories in Saudi Arabia and Azerbaijan. Red Bull's only defeat came in **Singapore**, where Sainz controlled the pace and used Lando Norris behind him to keep the faster Mercedes cars out of DRS range.

Behind Red Bull, the order changed several times:

- **Alonso** took six podiums in the opening eight races and made Aston Martin the early surprise.
- **McLaren's** Austria upgrade changed its season. Norris became a regular podium threat and **Oscar Piastri** won the Qatar Sprint in his rookie year.
- **Ferrari** remained inconsistent, but Sainz's Singapore drive prevented a clean Red Bull sweep.
- **Mercedes** won no races, yet its steadier points haul was enough to beat Ferrari to second in the Constructors' Championship.

## Points, Podiums, Sprints and DNFs

Verstappen missed the podium only once all year. His **21 podiums** were more than twice Pérez's nine; Alonso had eight and Norris seven. The numbers also show how quickly McLaren caught Aston Martin after its slow start.

## Point Progression

Pérez was still only six points behind Verstappen after winning in Azerbaijan. He did not win again. Verstappen took the next seven races, then extended that run to a record ten consecutive victories at Monza. The title was mathematically his during the Qatar Sprint, with six Grands Prix still to run.

## Conclusions and Final Standings

Verstappen ended the season with **575 points**, 290 more than Pérez, and Red Bull won 21 of the 22 completed Grands Prix. It was an extraordinary level of control.

The rest of the grid still moved forward. Alonso gave Aston Martin its strongest season, McLaren found a competitive car halfway through the year, and Piastri immediately looked at home in Formula 1. Sainz's Singapore victory remains the race that stopped Red Bull from completing a perfect sweep.
