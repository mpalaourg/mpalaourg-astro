Three drivers arrived in Abu Dhabi with a chance of becoming World Champion. **Max Verstappen** won the race, **Oscar Piastri** finished second, and **Lando Norris** took the third place he needed. After 24 Grands Prix and six Sprints, Norris won his first title by only **two points**.

It was not the route McLaren would have expected. The team had the fastest car for much of the year and comfortably retained the Constructors' Championship, but Piastri and Norris took points from one another while Verstappen recovered from a 104-point deficit after the Dutch Grand Prix. The result was Formula 1's closest Drivers' Championship since 2008.

## Race Calendar

The championship again covered **24 rounds**, from Australia in March to Abu Dhabi in December, with no cancellations. China, Miami, Belgium, the United States, São Paulo, and Qatar hosted the six Sprint sessions.

## Drivers and Constructors

After an unusually quiet 2024, the 2025 grid changed almost everywhere.

- **Lewis Hamilton** left Mercedes to join **Ferrari**, creating the most high-profile driver transfer of the season.
- **Carlos Sainz** moved to **Williams** after losing his Ferrari seat.
- **Andrea Kimi Antonelli** stepped into Hamilton's place at **Mercedes**.
- **Liam Lawson** began the season at **Red Bull Racing**, while **Isack Hadjar** joined **Racing Bulls**.
- **Esteban Ocon** and **Oliver Bearman** formed a new pairing at **Haas**.
- **Gabriel Bortoleto** debuted for **Kick Sauber**, and **Jack Doohan** started the year with **Alpine**.

The changes continued after the opening race. **Yuki Tsunoda** replaced Lawson at Red Bull from Japan, Lawson returned to Racing Bulls, and **Franco Colapinto** took over Jack Doohan's Alpine seat from Imola.

## 2025 Season Analysis

McLaren won **14 of the 24 Grands Prix**, split evenly between Norris and Piastri. Verstappen won eight for Red Bull and **George Russell** added two for Mercedes. Ferrari, despite the attention surrounding **Lewis Hamilton's** arrival, did not win a Grand Prix.

The numbers also explain why the Drivers' title became complicated. McLaren had the best two-car result, but its victories and podiums were divided almost evenly. Verstappen had no team-mate taking wins from him and remained close enough to punish every McLaren mistake.

Several results shaped the championship:

- **Norris** finally converted McLaren's pace into a world title, but only by surviving an aggressive late-season Verstappen run.
- **Piastri** won seven races and remained a genuine part of the championship picture for much of the year, giving McLaren a stronger two-car challenge than any rival.
- **Russell** delivered both Mercedes wins, in **Canada** and **Singapore**, while rookie **Antonelli** reached the podium three times.
- **Hamilton** won the Sprint in China but did not reach a Grand Prix podium during his first Ferrari season.
- **Williams** finished fifth, with two podiums for **Sainz** and much more consistent scoring from **Albon**.

## Points, Podiums, Sprints and DNFs

Norris led the podium count with **18**, followed by Piastri on 16 and Verstappen on 15. Together, the McLaren drivers occupied 34 of the 72 available podium places. Russell reached the top three nine times, while Leclerc managed seven in a Ferrari that never won a Grand Prix.

## Point Progression

Norris led after Australia, but Piastri's run of four wins in five races moved him ahead by Saudi Arabia. After winning at Zandvoort, Piastri held the largest lead of the year and Verstappen sat **104 points** behind him.

The final third changed everything. Verstappen won five of the last eight races, Piastri went six consecutive rounds without a podium, and Norris recovered the McLaren lead with victories in Mexico and São Paulo. The double disqualification in Las Vegas pulled Verstappen even closer. All three reached Abu Dhabi with a mathematical chance; Norris's third place settled it by two points.

## Conclusions and Final Standings

Norris became World Champion with **423 points**, only two ahead of Verstappen and 13 ahead of Piastri. His margin was the smallest since Lewis Hamilton beat Felipe Massa by one point in 2008.

McLaren's second consecutive Constructors' title was much clearer. Its **833 points** were 364 more than Mercedes, with Red Bull a further 18 behind. The team had dominated the year; the surprise was that its two drivers and a relentless Verstappen still carried the individual contest to the final laps.
